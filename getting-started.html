01 / Your Domain & Identity

          
Start with your name.

          

            Your domain is the address customers know. Register it—or keep
            it—in an account your business controls before anything goes live.
          

        

        

          

            
Your business email comes first.

            

              DNS changes can interrupt email if existing records are removed or
              replaced. Before touching nameservers, identify the service that
              runs your business mailbox and preserve its MX, SPF, DKIM, and
              DMARC records.
            

          

          

            You do not need to transfer your domain registration to Cloudflare.
            Keeping it at your current registrar is fine. You also do not need
            to give us your registrar password, recovery codes, or
            authentication codes.
          

          

            
              Detailed setup: domain and registrarOpen
            
            

              
    
                
  1. 
                  
    Register a new domain, or keep the one you have
    
                  
    
                    A new domain can be registered with any reputable registrar,
                    including
                    Cloudflare Registrar. An existing domain can stay exactly where it is. Moving a
                    registration is optional and is not part of this project.
                  
    
                
    2. 
                
  2. 
                  
    Keep the account and billing in your name
    
                  
    
                    The registrar account should belong to the business. Use an
                    email address you will keep, keep the renewal date and
                    payment method current, and decide whether auto-renew is
                    appropriate. You pay the registrar directly.
                  
    
                
    3. 
                
  3. 
                  
    Protect the account before you share access
    
                  
    
                    Use a unique password, enable multifactor authentication,
                    keep an independent recovery email and phone number, and
                    store recovery codes somewhere safe. You are responsible for
                    these credentials.
                  
    
                
    4. 
                
  4. 
                  
    Prepare DNS without breaking email
    
                  
    
                    Write down which provider runs your business mailbox and
                    which records it uses. When a domain is added to Cloudflare,

                    complete.

                    with the access described in the next section. We will

                    changed. You keep ownership of the domain and the account.

02 / Development Accounts

          
The accounts behind the work.

          

            These are the working accounts for your project. You create them,
            own them, and pay for them. We receive the access needed to build
            and maintain the result.
          

        

        

          

            
Before you invite anyone

            

              Use the account details in your engagement email. This project's
              repository is owned by the GitHub account
              hmarquardt. We do not publish an invitation email
              address here; if you do not have the invitation identity, ask Hank
              before sending an invitation.
            

            

              Never send passwords, multifactor codes, recovery codes, payment
              card details, or private API tokens. Invite the account; do not
              hand over the keys to it.

GitHub

              

              
Required Free to start

What it is for

                
Source code, changes, automation, and project history.

Cloudflare

              

              
Required Free plan available

            

            

              Cloudflare hosts websites and applications and can provide DNS,

What it is for

What it costs

Costs & your responsibilities

            

              You control the provider subscriptions, payment methods, renewals,
              usage budgets, spending limits, account security, and the decision
              to continue or cancel each service. We do not make financial
              decisions for you or configure spending limits automatically.
            

            

              

                
Alerts

                

                  Alerts notify you when usage or a balance crosses a threshold.
                  They help you act early, but they do not prevent an overage by
                  themselves.
                

              

              

                
Hard spending limits

                

                  A hard limit blocks requests once the cap is reached. That can
                  protect your budget—and can also interrupt a working
                  application. Choose limits with expected usage in mind.
                

              

            

            

              Alerts and budgets are useful together: alerts give you time to
              decide, and a reasonably sized hard limit prevents a surprise. No
              control eliminates all possibility of overage.

04 / Backups & Continuity

          
Plan recovery before you need it.

          

            If an application stores customer records, bookings, documents, or
            other persistent data, a backup plan belongs in the design—not in a
            later emergency.
          

        

        

          

            Our conceptual baseline comes from the backup design we use for our
            own CFLab environment. The exact implementation is sized to each
            project, but the principles are consistent:
          

          
    
            
  • 
              Automated backupsScheduled copies run without someone remembering to do them.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Rolling retentionRecent copies are kept on a defined schedule, not just the
                latest one.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Protected backup storageBackups live separately from production, so deleting or
                replacing production data does not delete the copy.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Integrity verificationBackups are checked so a corrupt or incomplete copy is caught
                before it is trusted.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Documented restorationThe recovery steps are written down and available to you, not
                held only in someone's memory.
            
    • 
            
  • 
              Periodic restoration testsA restore is demonstrated on a schedule. An untested backup is
                only a hope.
            
    • 
          

          

            “A backup is not operational until a restore has been demonstrated.”
          

          

            
Backups are not guaranteed business continuity.

            

              A standard backup design covers common problems such as an
              accidental deletion, a bad deployment, or a corrupted record. It
              cannot promise that every event is survivable. A Cloudflare-wide
              outage, loss of the provider account, catastrophic provider
              failure, or a coordinated attack may exceed the design.
            

            

              We will not promise a particular recovery time, recovery point,
              retention period, or service guarantee unless we agree to it
              separately in writing.
            

          

          

            Initial backup setup is part of the technical work we handle after
            the handoff. Ongoing monitoring, maintenance, restoration assistance,
            and any contractual service levels are established separately from
            this guide.

05 / Ownership & Changing Developers

          
You're Never Locked In.

          

            You can work with another developer whenever you choose. Ownership
            makes that possible; planning makes it clean.
          

        

        

          

            A developer transition should not require moving accounts away from
            a consultant. Your domain, code, hosting, and billing are already
            yours. The work is handing over the operational knowledge and closing
            the access that is no longer needed.
          

          
    
            
  1. 
              
    Invite the replacement developer
    
              
    
                Add them to the relevant services with the access they need. Keep
                your own owner access throughout.
              
    
            
    2. 
            
  2. 
              
    Provide repository and operational documentation
    
              
    
                Share the code repository, deployment notes, environment
                inventory, backup and restore procedures, and the location of
                credentials—never the credentials themselves in plain text.
              
    
            
    3. 
            
  3. 
              
    Confirm the new developer can build and deploy
    
              
    
                Have them run the build, tests, and a deployment before the
                previous developer steps away. Access is not the same as a
                working handoff.
              
    
            
    4. 
            
  4. 
              
    Transfer maintenance and operational responsibilities
    
              
    
                Make it explicit who watches backups, renewals, alerts, security
                updates, and support requests from this point forward.
              
    
            
    5. 
            
  5. 
              
    Remove the previous developer's memberships
    
              
    
                Remove their account from GitHub, Cloudflare, OpenRouter, and any
                other service where they had access. Confirm the removal took
                effect.
              
    
            
    6. 
            
  6. 
              
    Rotate keys, tokens, secrets, and integrations
    
              
    
                Replace deployment tokens, API keys, CI/CD secrets, webhooks, and
                OAuth connections that the previous developer created or could
                use. Removal from an account does not automatically invalidate a
                credential they already hold.
              
    
            
    7. 
            
  7. 
              
    Verify production and automation still work
    
              
    
                Test the live site or application, the deployment pipeline,
                scheduled jobs, backups, and any integrations. Fix or document
                anything that breaks during the transition.
              
    
            
    8. 
          

          

            
Removing access is not the same as revoking every key.

            

              Account membership and credentials are different things. A former
              collaborator may still hold a personal access token, deploy key,
              API token, OAuth authorization, webhook signing secret, or an
              integration they created. Review each provider's token and
              integration settings, then rotate what remains valid.
            

            

              Remove a GitHub member
              Manage GitHub access tokens
              Remove a Cloudflare member
              Manage Cloudflare API tokens
              Manage OpenRouter keys
            

          

          

            This guidance is available from the beginning on purpose. You should
            know how a transition works before you need one.
